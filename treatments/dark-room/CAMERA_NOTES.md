# Dark Room - Camera / Edit Notes

Artist: Mr Star City
Track: Dark Room
Runtime: 2:45
Purpose: companion notes for shot language, edit rhythm, transitions, and camera behavior. Keep the main treatment focused on scene description only.

## 00:00-00:12 - Penthouse Terrace

Elegant, controlled luxury-commercial language. Start wide enough to feel the terrace and sunrise, then push closer on the mimosa sip, the glass landing, the untouched omelette, the cigar, and the MSC robe.

## 00:13-00:19 - Bedroom Ritual

Use close inserts on watch, shoes, gun, waistband, robe detail, and cigar smoke. Medium profile, mirror, and reflection angles should dominate. Avoid direct-to-camera performance here; Mal performs to himself.

## 00:19-00:25 - Stairs / Door / Look Back

Start wide from the bottom floor looking up as Mal descends through the architecture. Cut to closer tracking as he walks down. Hold the over-the-shoulder look back to the couch and table before the visual shift begins.

## 00:25-00:33 - First Hook / Couch Portal

Use an over-the-shoulder look back, then push toward the couch/table overlay. Move into a close narrative view of past Mal on the couch.

## 00:33-00:39 - Condo Melt / Dark Room Business

Let the condo melt into locked overhead fisheye blackness, then hard cut back to narrative trap-house action, ending at the bedroom window/blinds.

## 00:39-00:46 - Blinds / Blackout / Hit The Lights

Close on fingers through blinds, side profile at the window, dust in the light, then slow dolly toward the beam. Dip to black. Cut to bulb close-up, switch close-up, floor-level cockroach scatter, then fisheye wide.

## 00:46-00:53 - Serving / Avoiding The Law

Fragment the geography. Use tight hands, partial faces, door edge, peephole or chain insert, cash hitting table, and Mal's quick glance toward the door. Keep it crowded and unsafe.

## 00:53-01:07 - Water / Rain / Survival Shift

Use a macro liquid insert as the transition into reflection and rain. Move through rain glass, wet pavement, handheld flashback fragments, and then hard cut back to Mal in the room. Keep the seizure/pain imagery impressionistic, not explanatory.

## 01:07-01:20 - Hook Return / Static Overhead Fisheye

Locked static tripod. Hype Williams-style fisheye looking straight down into pure black. One hanging bulb. Use the frame as a cut-effect machine: extras enter and vanish, props appear and disappear, bodies crowd Mal while he stays centered. Intercut one-frame flashes of the luxury apartment.

## 01:20-01:31 - Star On The J Train

Let this breathe after the claustrophobic dark room. Use train-window reflections, profile close-ups, exterior bridge flashes, hands holding film/envelope, and golden sunset light across Star's face.

## 01:31-01:39 - Childhood Dreams / Status Objects

Use match cuts between train reflections and apartment reflections, watch glints, bridge steel, marble, blue hundreds, cigar ember, and photo negatives. Let Star's reflection briefly distort toward the darker memory world.

## 01:39-01:56 - Crack Montage / Physical Cost

Use locked overhead plus hard inserts. Keep the table rhythm ritualistic: baggies hit, cash snaps in, scale numbers flash, blade/card motion, smoke into bulb light. Cut to feet/shoes for the standing-too-long cue. For the burning-lip-skin image, stay close and sensory without explaining the action.

## 01:56-02:13 - Film Printer / Photos Developed

Keep the photo lab calm and procedural. Use hands, envelope, prints, machine details, red/amber practical light, and Star's eyes scanning the images. The calmness should make the impossible photos more unsettling.

## 02:13-02:26 - Polaroids / Evidence Loop

Use freeze-frame pops and print matches. A moving fisheye frame freezes, then appears as a print in Star's hand. Repeat with the couch, table, and luxury-condo details. Emphasize Polaroid texture and physical evidence.

## 02:26-02:45 - Final Hook / Image That Sticks

Build in steps: Mal reaches the apartment door again, Star sees Mal under the bulb in a photo, Mal looks into the fisheye, then the luxury apartment gets one last dark-room overlay. The final shot should land as a clean, memorable image rather than a complicated montage.
