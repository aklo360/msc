## Format Notes

This is a mobile-first, shot-by-shot, moment-by-moment treatment format. It combines:

- Treatment prose: present-tense, visual, readable, and focused on tone, concept, and story turns.
- Shot-list discipline: each visual moment gets a shot number, shot size, camera movement, subject, location, and production/post notes.
- Timecode discipline: every entry is tied to a music time range so the video can be workshopped against the track.
- Music-video sync: scene changes, transformations, cut density, and performance energy should respond to verse/hook/bridge structure, beat/downbeat emphasis, emotional climaxes, and off-beat surprises.

Mobile rules:

- No tables.
- Short headings.
- One visual idea per entry.
- Use compact labels: `Cue`, `Visual`, `Camera`, `Notes`.
- Keep paragraphs under 3 lines when possible.
- Do not paste full lyrics. Use short lyric cues or paraphrased cue names until exact lyric usage is cleared for the final treatment.

## Research Principles Behind This Template

- A treatment should communicate important scenes, story points, tone, and directorial style in present-tense prose, while staying readable and economic.
- A shot list should include the shot number, description, shot size/type, camera movement, subject, location, equipment or lens notes, VFX, lighting, and production notes.
- Timecode should be consistent. For this writing draft, use `MM:SS-MM:SS`; if this becomes an edit-facing document, convert key moments to SMPTE `HH:MM:SS:FF` once frame rate is chosen.
- For music videos, the music structure matters: verses, hooks, bridges, beats, downbeats, and emotional climaxes should drive cuts, transformations, performance intensity, and scene transitions.
- Text-based audiovisual scripts are useful because they let the team navigate visuals, speech/lyrics, and timeline logic together before cutting.

## Header / One-Page Concept

### Logline

A music video that juxtaposes Mal and Star in current-day luxury with visions of their past in a dark, dingy trap-house world that keeps invading the present.

### Core Visual Engine

Luxury surfaces transform into dirty past-tense versions of the same spaces and objects. The past is not shown as a clean flashback. It breaks into the present and takes over.

### Current Character Logic

- Mal opens alone in the penthouse/luxury-apartment world.
- Star enters later through the club/nightlife thread.
- Both performers eventually get pulled into the same dark-room memory logic.

### Visual Rules

- Present world: sunrise, silk robe, mimosa, French omelette, watch, polished wood, clean lines, high-floor space, controlled camera.
- Past world: harsh overhead light, dirty couch, disheveled table, baggies, scales, coke, stale liquor, cockroaches, pressure at the door.
- Transformation rule: start as overlays/reflections/match cuts, then become full environmental takeovers.
- Performance rule: performance continues across both timelines.
- Memory rule: the past is invasive, not archival.

## Timecode Entries

Use one compact entry per visual beat. Each entry should be easy to edit on a phone.

### 00:00-00:08 - Shot 1 - Penthouse Sunrise

Visual: Mal sits at sunrise on a penthouse rooftop in a silk robe, mimosa and omelette on the table. Quiet, expensive, calm.

Cue: intro / opening pressure.

Camera: wide to medium, locked or very slow push.

Notes: establish luxury before any darkness appears.

### 00:08-00:16 - Shot 2 - Terrace Into Bedroom

Visual: Mal stands and crosses from the private terrace into the bedroom. Luxury feels routine.

Cue: grown status begins meeting danger.

Camera: smooth tracking medium through the doorway.

### 00:16-00:25 - Shot 3 - Watch And Gun

Visual: Mal takes a watch and gun from the dresser. Watch on wrist. Gun in waistband. First crack in the calm.

Cue: protection, talent, survival pressure.

Camera: controlled inserts and close-ups.

Notes: watch and gun should feel like equal status/survival objects.

### 00:25-00:35 - Shot 4 - Downstairs / Robe To Jacket

Visual: Mal moves downstairs through the multi-floor apartment. On the way out, robe becomes jacket.

Cue: leaving luxury.

Camera: medium/wide Steadicam or dolly.

Notes: transition should feel stylish, not fully magical yet.

### 00:35-00:45 - Shot 5 - Foyer Look Back

Visual: Mal stops before leaving and looks back at the pristine couch and wooden coffee table.

Cue: hook approach.

Camera: medium on Mal into POV or slow push.

Notes: give the audience a clean read before the objects transform.

### 00:45-00:58 - Shot 6 - First Transformation

Visual: The past overlays the clean foyer. Couch and table become their old trap-house versions. The pristine room turns dirty.

Cue: first hook transformation.

Camera: wide, locked or slow moving.

Notes: first major visual thesis. Use overlay, flicker, match dissolve, and practical dirt swap.

### 00:58-01:10 - Shot 7 - Trap-House Work

Visual: Table now covered with scales, baggies, coke, and mess. Past Mal works on the couch while performing.

Cue: trap-house labor / survival routine.

Camera: handheld creeps in, mixed with inserts.

Notes: cutting can get faster and less clean.

### 01:10-01:20 - Shot 8 - Door Pressure

Visual: The room reacts to pressure at the door. Mal performs through stress, need, danger, and interruption.

Cue: door-pressure lyric moment.

Camera: close-ups, door insert, rack focus, handheld instability.

Notes: one of the most vivid danger beats.

### 01:20-01:32 - Shot 9 - Star Enters Through Club

Visual: Star enters through the club world. He arrives with nightlife energy, separate from Mal's penthouse opening.

Cue: club thread setup.

Camera: medium/wide, handheld or flowing through bodies.

Notes: decide whether Star is calm, watched, already haunted, or fully inside the nightlife surface.

### 01:32-01:45 - Shot 10 - Club Starts Becoming Dark Room

Visual: Club starts echoing trap-house imagery: red light, cramped bodies, glass/liquor, faces at frame edge.

Cue: club-to-dark-room pull.

Camera: handheld, whip pans, crowded frame edges.

Notes: Star starts getting pulled into Mal's memory logic.

### 01:45-02:00 - Shot 11 - Childhood / Luxury Contrast

Visual: Watches, childhood dreams, old photos, and status objects appear as reflections or printed images.

Cue: childhood dreams / adult luxury markers.

Camera: inserts, close-ups, static macro, slow push.

Notes: connect what they wanted as kids to what the past cost.

### 02:00-02:15 - Shot 12 - Photo Development Motif

Visual: Title becomes literal: red safe-light, trays, contact sheets, images developing in liquid or on walls.

Cue: photo-development / darkroom image.

Camera: controlled ritual close-ups widening into reveal.

Notes: practical trays, projections, liquid transitions.

### 02:15-02:30 - Shot 13 - Worlds Converge

Visual: Club, trap house, and luxury spaces converge. Present can no longer contain the past.

Cue: final build / convergence.

Camera: wider performance tableau, more kinetic.

Open: do Mal and Star end in the same transformed space?

### 02:30-02:45 - Shot 14 - Final Image

Visual: The room disappears, stays, or follows them into the present. End on the image that sticks.

Cue: final hook / final image.

Camera: TBD.

Open: does the past release, remain, or become portable?
