# **Dark Room (Official Music Video)**

**Artist: Manhattan Mal & Mr Star City  | Runtime: 2:45  | Version: v01 07/01/26**

## **Synopsis**

**Dark Room is a music video about success being haunted by survival. Mal begins in a clean, expensive present-day world in a luxury condo in Manhattan. As the song turns, the space is transformed by flashes of the past: a dirty trap-house room, overlays and juxtaposes the pristine condo interior as memories refuse to stay buried.**

**The video moves between three core scenes. First is the two part master scene, Mal's glossy present-day luxury world with the flashbacks of the trap house past interwoven. Second is a literal dark-room void performance scene: a black room with one single hanging lightbulb where Mal performs while props, extras & montage details shift & transform around him. Third is Star's city B-story: riding the J train at sunset over the Williamsburg Bridge on the way to get film developed. When Star reaches the photo lab and looks through the prints, the photographs become evidence of the same haunted past.**

**By the end, the "dark room" means all three things at once: the room they survived, the photo lab where images develop, and the psychological space that still follows them into success.**

## **Treatment**

### **00:00-00:12 \- Penthouse Terrace**

**Mal sits on the penthouse terrace in a silk robe designed by MSC, covered in his signature art style. He watches the sunrise with the city below him. A cigar sits nearby, adding old-money calm and smoke texture.**

**He lifts a mimosa, takes a sip, then sets the glass down beside a pristine untouched French omelette. The food looks almost too perfect. After setting the glass down, he rises and walks inside toward the bedroom.**

### **00:13-00:19 \- Bedroom Ritual**

**Mal continues through the bedroom while performing the track to himself, not into camera. He is inside his own morning ritual, not presenting to us yet.**

**He puts on his watch. He puts on his shoes. He grabs the gun. He places it into his waistband. The watch, shoes, and gun are part of the same routine: success, presentation, and survival.**

### **00:19-00:25 \- Stairs / Door / Look Back**

**Mal walks down the stairs into the foyer, we see close ups of his flowing clothing & jewelry as he walks down in slow motion.**

**At the ground floor on the dark-things lyric, he stops. He looks back over his shoulder at the foyer: plastic-covered couch, pristine antique luxury coffee table, polished surfaces, perfect stillness.**

**At this moment the first flash back happens.**

### **00:25-00:39 \- First Hook / Couch Portal**

**The couch and coffee table become the connection point between the luxury condo and the trap house of the past. The plastic-covered luxury couch becomes a dirty beat up couch that Mal’s former self sits on. The antique coffee table becomes a dingy mess with scales, baggies & work on it.**

**We punch in on this scene of Mal on the couch performing the hook, still not to camera. He is looking straight off, locked into the room, with the coffee table in front of him covered in scales, baggies, coke, cash, wrappers, sticky glass, ash, and stains.**

**Around 00:33, the condo starts to melt away. The only things still connecting the spaces are the couch and coffee table. Everything else falls into blackness until we arrive in the true dark-room void: pure black, one hanging bulb, Mal isolated in the frame.**

**On the fiends/table-work cue around 00:34, the abstract void gives way to the narrative past. Mal is no longer just performing the hook; he is back inside the past, serving work to fiends and moving through the room's business.**

**At 00:38, on the light-outside/dark-inside cue, Mal is now in the trap-house bedroom, rapping toward the window instead of camera. He puts his fingers through the blinds and pulls them slightly open. A hard beam of daylight peers into the room.**

### **00:39-00:46 \- Blinds / Blackout / Hit The Lights**

**Mal stays at the trap-house bedroom window, fingers still parting the blinds. He raps into the window light, not into camera. The beam becomes the cleanest thing in the room, cutting through dust and smoke.**

**Instead of saving him, the light gets swallowed. The darkness of his aura overcasts the sunlight coming through the blinds until the frame dips to pure black.**

**At the moment of pure black, the hanging bulb from the dark-room void flicks on. He hits a light switch. Cockroaches scatter across the dingy floor. Then we return to the wide dark-room setup: Mal under the bulb, now rapping the next couple lines directly to camera.**

### **00:46-00:53 \- Serving / Avoiding The Law**

**The narrative trap-house room becomes a workplace. People move at the edge of frame, never fully cleanly introduced. A hand knocks. Another hand pushes cash forward. Someone waits near the door.**

**Mal stays on the couch/table axis, moving with routine precision. On the serving/law cue, his eyes flick to the door before he completes the exchange. Every action has a check built into it: listen, look, move, serve, reset.**

### **00:53-01:07 \- Water / Rain / Survival Shift**

**Water becomes the transition out of the room's business. A glass, sink, or spilled liquid on the trap-house table expands into a street reflection. The room gives way to rain, wet pavement, and distorted reflections.**

**Mal appears in fragments: reflected in wet pavement, seen through rain-streaked glass, doubled in the apartment window, then back in the dark room. The visuals feel like he is being pushed through danger and still finding his footing.**

**On the pain/rain/seizure cue, the memory flashes quickly: someone buckling, hands reaching in, a small object hitting the floor, Mal registering it for a fraction of a second before the edit pulls away.**

### **01:07-01:20 \- Hook Return / Static Overhead Fisheye**

**The hook returns to the overhead dark-room setup. Mal is alone under the hanging bulb at first, rapping up into camera. Then the room starts changing around him in jumps.**

**One person enters. Then three bodies are suddenly around him. The table appears. The table disappears. Bodies crowd him and vanish again. Mal stays locked at the center while time seems to skip around him.**

**Brief flashes of the luxury apartment interrupt the sequence: the untouched omelette, the mimosa glass, the robe pattern, the front door knob, the antique table. The clean world tries and fails to interrupt the dark room.**

### **01:20-01:31 \- Star On The J Train**

**Star enters as the B-story after the dark room has fully landed. He is riding the J train at sunset, crossing the Williamsburg Bridge on the way to get film developed.**

**He is not in the dark room yet. He is in transit. The city passes through the train windows. The sunset hits the bridge steel. He has film, a claim ticket, or an envelope in hand. He looks out the window, but his reflection briefly shows something darker behind him.**

### **01:31-01:39 \- Childhood Dreams / Status Objects**

**As the song turns toward childhood dreams and adult status, Star's train ride intercuts with Mal's luxury world. Star watches the bridge and skyline. Mal's watch catches light. The blue hundreds sit on marble. Cigar smoke drifts through morning sun. The untouched omelette still sits perfect on the terrace.**

**The images feel expensive but haunted, like every status object carries a shadow. Star's train-window reflection briefly becomes younger, dirtier, or closer to the dark room.**

### **01:39-01:56 \- Crack Montage / Physical Cost**

**The coke, standing, flipping, and burning-lip-skin imagery drives the most intense montage. Product and cash details move in ritual rhythm: baggies hit the table, cash snaps in, scale numbers flash, smoke curls into the bulb light.**

**The room keeps changing around Mal. Bodies crowd in. The table appears, disappears, and returns messier. Feet shift on the floor from standing too long. The clean shoes from the bedroom are echoed by feet trapped in the grind.**

**On the burning-lip-skin image, the video becomes intimate and uncomfortable: heat, smoke, a flinch, cracked lips, and harsh light. The moment shows the physical cost of the room without explaining the mechanics.**

### **01:56-02:13 \- Film Printer / Photos Developed**

**Star reaches the film printer or photo lab. The dark-room title becomes literal. We see the machine, the counter, the envelope, and the prints. A hand slides the envelope over. Star opens it and starts looking.**

**At first, the photos feel normal: train windows, city, nightlife, motion blur, friends, fragments of the day. Then the images become wrong. They show the dirty couch, the old table, the hanging bulb, Mal under the dark-room light, younger shadows, and places Star was not just standing in.**

### **02:13-02:26 \- Polaroids / Evidence Loop**

**The overhead performance freezes into Polaroids. Mal under the bulb becomes a printed image. Star flips through the developed photos and finds frames that match the overhead performance and the trap-house room.**

**A moving dark-room image freezes, then appears as a print in Star's hand. A trap-house couch frame freezes, then appears in the stack. A luxury-condo detail freezes, then appears printed with grime that was not there before.**

**The three looks now connect directly: Mal's luxury apartment, Mal's overhead void, and Star's photo-lab prints. The past is no longer just remembered. It is physical evidence.**

### **02:26-02:45 \- Final Hook / Image That Sticks**

**The final hook crosscuts all three worlds with clarity. Mal in the luxury apartment. Mal under the hanging bulb. Star on the train. Star in the photo lab. The dirty room appears inside the clean apartment. The photographs show images that should not exist.**

**The ending builds in steps. Mal reaches the apartment door again, echoing the earlier doorway beat. Star looks down at a photo that shows Mal under the bulb. Mal under the bulb looks directly out from the darkness. The luxury apartment gets one last dark-room overlay.**

**Final image option one: Star holds a photo of Mal under the hanging bulb. He looks up, and the same bulb is now above him.**

**Final image option two: Mal leaves the apartment, but on the marble island we see the sliced money-cake beside a Polaroid of the dark room.**

**Either ending says the same thing: success does not erase the dark room. It only gives the past cleaner surfaces to develop on.**
