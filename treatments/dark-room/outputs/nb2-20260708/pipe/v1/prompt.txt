An extreme macro close-up of the glowing orange cherry of a small glass pipe in the dark, the ember burning hot amber and red at the very center of the frame, with a single thin ribbon of white smoke coiling straight up out of it into a hard narrow shaft of overhead tungsten light. Just at the soft edge of the frame, a pair of dry, cracked lips catches the ember's glow. Everything beyond the small pool of ember light falls away into deep, total black. The focus is razor-sharp on the glowing cherry, the smoke drifting upward in slow delicate curls, tiny sparks suspended in the rising heat. Lighting comes almost entirely from the ember itself and one hard sliver of tungsten light from above, warm orange bleeding softly into the surrounding darkness, an intimate and uncomfortable closeness to the heat.

Shot as a still frame from a 1990s golden-era hip hop music video crossed with Abel Ferrara's King of New York (1990): 35mm motion picture film, natural Kodak color, deep gentle shadows, subtle halation on the highlights, fine film grain, a glossy photographic print finish. The image fills the entire frame edge to edge, full-bleed.
