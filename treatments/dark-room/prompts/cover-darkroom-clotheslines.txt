A wide eye-level view of a photographic darkroom bathed in deep red safelight. Two taut horizontal clotheslines stretch across the full width of the frame, one along the upper third and one along the lower third, each strung with damp medium-format film prints held by wooden clothespins. The glossy prints have clean white borders, and each one holds a different developing scene: a silhouetted man with a cigar watching sunrise from a penthouse terrace, a man seated on a plastic-covered floral couch beneath a wall of framed family portraits, a single bare lightbulb hanging in pure blackness, the grand stone arch and colonnade of the Manhattan Bridge, a champagne bottle erupting in a slow-motion spray, and a shivering figure hunched on a winter street. Between the two rows the middle of the room falls away into soft black shadow, with faint red rim light tracing developing trays, tongs, and amber chemical bottles along the bottom edge of the frame. Captured on a 35mm lens at f/2.8 with shallow depth of field, fine film grain, crushed blacks, and a monochromatic deep red grade, the hanging prints glowing as the brightest surfaces in the room. The scene fills the entire frame edge to edge, full-bleed.
